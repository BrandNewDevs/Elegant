# Design

Name: **Elegant** (permanent). Domain vocabulary is in [`GLOSSARY.md`](../GLOSSARY.md). Decisions that are hard to reverse are in [`docs/adr/`](./adr/).

A personal macOS menu-bar app that notices when you are in an online call, offers to record it, and produces a transcript and summary entirely on your own machine. Single user. No calendar.

## Flow

1. **Detection.** The Swift engine sees an app holding the microphone. Any process mapped to a `.app` bundle counts (Zoom, Teams, Slack, FaceTime, Meet in any browser, and so on) except built-in ignores (Siri, dictation-style apps such as Wispr Flow, and others in `app/src/core/detection.ts`) and apps listed in `ignored-apps.json` (`{ "ignoredApps": [...] }`, opened from the menu bar's "Ignored apps…"). Browsers will sometimes cause false prompts; the Prompt is cheap to dismiss, so that is accepted.
   The engine reports each process that uses the microphone, mapped to the outermost app in its executable path; the app folds helper processes into one app and applies a debounce (default 2 s, in `app/src/core/detection.ts`, to be tuned in the demo pass). The ignore file is read again at each microphone event, so an edit applies at once; an unreadable file keeps the last good list. A declined Meeting is not asked about again until the app releases the microphone. Whether Safari and FaceTime, whose audio may run in system processes outside any `.app`, are reported at all is untested; see the open risks.
2. **Prompt.** An overlay offers **Record**, **Not now** and **Never for this app**. Never is stored in SQLite (`neverApps`) and that app is not detected or buffered again; the menu bar's "Never prompt for" submenu lists such apps and choosing one undoes it. The Prompt times out after about 20 seconds and defaults to **not recording**. From Detection onward the app holds the last few minutes of audio in memory; accepting keeps them, declining or ignoring discards them. Nothing is stored for a declined Meeting. At Detection the app sends `start_buffer`; the engine captures both tracks into memory only (the last 5 minutes, `preBufferSeconds` in `engine/Sources/engine/PCMBuffer.swift`). `start_recording` flushes that to the start of the Recording, and `discard_buffer` drops it. If the engine cannot buffer (for example microphone access is denied), `start_recording` starts from that moment instead and reports its own failure.
3. **Recording.** The microphone (you) and system audio (everyone else) are captured as two separate tracks. A menu-bar indicator shows elapsed time. **Stop is manual only.** When the call app releases the microphone the app shows a notification as a reminder (`AppCore.onStopNudge`), but never stops by itself.
4. **After Stop.** Transcription runs once over the full recording (not live), English only. Diarization labels the other voices "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2", and so on; the user can rename them. The engine diarizes the system track with SpeakerKit (Pyannote) after transcribing it: each segment goes to the voice that speaks longest within it, and voices are numbered by when they are first heard, so labels are stable within a Meeting (`engine/Sources/engine/SpeakerLabelling.swift`). Renames are per Meeting and stored on the Participant; the stored Transcript and Summary keep labels, and names are substituted at display (`app/src/core/participants.ts`). A local open-weight model via Ollama (default `gemma3:4b`) writes the Summary: a TL;DR plus what each Participant said. Summary generation now keeps supporting Transcript references and generation metadata alongside the existing TL;DR and per-Participant points. Long Meetings use mandatory original-sentence classification and bounded consolidation. The Meetings window now offers Summary, Transcript and Chat views, with locally saved questions and answers and clickable supporting timestamps.
5. **Audio.** Deleted after a successful transcription. The "Keep audio after transcription" setting (menu bar, stored in SQLite, off by default) keeps it; deleting a Meeting removes kept audio too.

## App

- SQLite is the source of truth, with full-text search over Transcripts and Summaries.
- v1 features: Meeting list and detail view, Transcript with Participant labels, Summary, rename a Participant, delete a Meeting and all its data, regenerate a Summary, export a Meeting to Markdown with Summary evidence timestamps, Meeting chat with saved history, clickable references, search.
- Launches at login. Models download on first run. First run also walks through the macOS permission prompts (microphone, system-audio capture) and checks that Ollama is running with a model pulled.
- Setup (`app/src/setup/setup.ts`) is shown in two places, both served by `app/src/main/setup-bridge.ts`. Onboarding (`onboarding-window.ts`) is its own small window that asks for the person's name (optional), then walks through the two macOS permissions, one screen each, and opens at launch only when a permission is missing. On a first launch it is the only window: the Meetings window waits until the engine has reported the permissions and then opens when onboarding closes, whether the person finished it or closed it early (Settings still shows what is missing). A login launch opens onboarding when a permission is missing and no Meetings window either way. Settings is a dialog over the main window: "Settings…" in the menu bar (⌘,) or the Settings button in the sidebar opens it, and Esc or the close button dismisses it. It holds the "Show in Dock" switch, shows each permission, and holds the speech and speaker model download and the Ollama check; neither of those blocks onboarding or recording, and a missing model downloads on first use. Each permission explains itself and is requested only when its button is pressed, so no macOS prompt appears unannounced. The person's name lives on a local Profile page instead: the Profile button in the sidebar swaps the main area to it, and picking a Meeting swaps back. It is stored as the `userName` setting and shown in place of "You" in every Meeting, the Summary and exports, unless that Meeting renamed You. Nothing else is kept in a profile, and nothing leaves the Mac.
- The app lives in the menu bar, and the menu bar icon is always there. The "Show in Dock" setting (Settings and the menu bar, stored in SQLite as `showInDock`, on unless the person turned it off) also gives it a Dock icon: clicking the icon opens the main window, and minimised windows go to the Dock. It applies at once and at each launch. The packaged Info.plist keeps `LSUIElement`, so the app starts with no Dock icon and never flashes one when the setting is off; with it on, which is the default, the app calls `app.dock.show()` at launch, which turns it into a regular app while it runs, and `app.dock.hide()` turns it back. A stored `false` is kept, so only people who never chose get the default. Turning it off leaves open windows open.
- A granted permission is remembered in the store (`permission.microphone`, `permission.system_audio`) and checked again, without a prompt, when the engine reports `ready` and whenever the Settings dialog opens, so the person is asked once. The engine answers `check_permission` with `permission_result`; the microphone is read from its macOS status. System audio has no macOS call that reports its status, so the engine starts a tap briefly and counts a running tap as granted; that prompts if the person never answered, so the app sends it only for a permission it recorded as granted. A permission that was granted and is now off is reported as a problem with where to turn it back on. `request_permission` answers a permission that was already answered without a prompt, so "Check again" after fixing it in System Settings works. The models are remembered the same way (`modelsReady`).
- Bundle identifier: `dev.bnd.elegant`. It is permanent because macOS ties permissions and the login item to it; the display name lives in one config.
- `pnpm run package [identity]` (`app/scripts/package.sh`) builds `app/out/<display name>.app` from Electron's own bundle: the executable and helper apps take the display name (Electron counts as packaged only when its executable is not called Electron), the app goes in `Contents/Resources/app` and the engine in `Contents/Helpers/engine`, and everything is signed inside-out. macOS ties the microphone and system-audio grants to the signing identity, so a rebuild keeps them only when signed with the same one; ad-hoc signing (the default, `-`) re-prompts after every rebuild. A self-signed code-signing certificate from Keychain Access is enough; no Apple Developer account is needed for the person's own Mac. The packaged app registers itself as a login item while macOS reports it as never registered (`not-found` or `not-registered`), and a run from source never registers. Turning it off in System Settings is expected to report `requires-approval` and so stay off; that is not yet checked on the machine.
- Releases are built by the Release workflow (`.github/workflows/release.yml`) on a macOS runner. Pushing a `v<version>` tag that matches `app/package.json` runs typecheck and the tests, builds the signed `.dmg` (`app/scripts/dmg.sh`: an installer window with a drag-to-Applications arrow, laid out by dmgbuild from `app/scripts/dmg-settings.py` and the background in `app/assets/`, which `app/scripts/dmg-background.swift` draws; dmgbuild needs no Finder, so it works headless on the runner, and the workflow installs it with pip) and attaches it to a draft GitHub release (`app/scripts/release.sh`); publish the draft once it has been checked. The draft's notes are a template in `release.sh` (install steps including the macOS 15 "Open Anyway" location and the `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine` fallback, a permissions note, requirements) followed by the "What's Changed" list GitHub generates (`gh release create --generate-notes`) from the pull requests merged since the previous release, or all of them for a first release; that list comes from GitHub, so the checkout needs no tag history. Running the workflow by hand builds the same `.dmg` as a workflow artifact, with no release. CI signs with the same self-signed certificate as local builds, because the app's designated requirement pins it: the `release` environment holds it as `SIGNING_IDENTITY_P12` (the base64 of a `.p12` exported from Keychain Access) and `SIGNING_IDENTITY_PASSWORD`, `app/scripts/import-signing-identity.sh` loads it into a keychain that lasts for the job, and the workflow fails if its hash is not the one in `SIGNING_IDENTITY_SHA1`. macOS does not trust the certificate, and `codesign` signs with it anyway. The environment should allow only `v*` tags and `main`, so no other branch can reach the key. There is no notarization: that needs an Apple Developer ID, so a first install still needs "Open Anyway" (macOS 15 and later: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Security).

## Shape

A headless Swift engine (detection, two-track capture, WhisperKit, diarization) reports over stdio as JSON to an Electron/React app that owns the window, the Prompt overlay, SQLite and the Ollama call. One signed `.app`. See [ADR 0001](./adr/0001-swift-engine-electron-ui.md) and [ADR 0002](./adr/0002-fully-local-processing.md).

## AI delivery scope

The AI delivery targets English Meetings up to 60 minutes, in two milestones: evidence-backed Summaries first, then chat about the selected Meeting. Summary generation, shared request infrastructure, Meeting-specific evidence retrieval, persisted Meeting chat and React integration are implemented. The target duration and quality gates still require evaluation on reserved examples and the M4. TypeScript owns production AI logic, Swift owns transcription and diarization, Ollama serves Gemma locally, and Python is evaluation-only. Python is not shipped with the app.

- A Summary keeps its existing TL;DR and per-Participant points. Optional evidence and generation sidecars are stored atomically alongside it. Each factual point and the TL;DR link to supporting Transcript segments. Existing Summaries remain readable; regeneration adds evidence.
- References identify a segment by its ordinal within a fingerprinted Transcript revision. Renaming a Participant changes display names, never reference identity. Timestamp buttons in the TL;DR, per-Participant points and chat open, focus and highlight the referenced Transcript passage; retained audio is unnecessary.
- Long Meetings classify every original sentence, followed by per-speaker topic grouping of immutable clauses. Application code maps an exact-length ordered classification array to original sentences and supplies their words and ownership; malformed, missing or extra classifications fail validation. The reducer proposes related-topic groups; it cannot author replacement text, move ownership or replace citations. Conservative detail checks permit one unchanged representative for equivalent clauses while preserving every original leaf and reference; changed details remain visible. Original leaf claims and their source passages remain available, while the short TL;DR selects whole extracted claims from ranked complete bundles that fit its own request. Application code copies those claims and their speaker labels rather than asking for a new factual compression. This favors retaining distinct facts over aggressive shortening; extraction and TL;DR accuracy still require review. Incomplete processing fails clearly, preserving any previous Summary.
- Chat answers questions about one selected Meeting using original Transcript fragments. English answers quote original fragments surrounding selected sentences with their actual speakers and cite segment ordinals. It resolves follow-up subjects from the previous user question, tries one complete-scan fallback when targeted context cannot answer, and validates selected source IDs and the current revision before publication. The model selects canonical original sentence IDs and code supplies their whole fragments, actual speakers and citations; semantic relevance still uses model judgment; narrow person and date/budget value checks prevent known substitutions. It distinguishes verification abstention and incomplete review from a completed review finding no support, and never uses the Summary or earlier answers as factual evidence. Malformed output and operational failures remain failed requests. A valid citation proves source identity, not that the selection answers the question.
- SQLite saves the full chat history per Meeting, including queued, retrieving, generating, completed, failed, cancelled and interrupted requests. The React Chat view sends questions, shows queued/retrieving/generating and terminal statuses, and offers Cancel, Retry and Clear chat. The local AI queue shows queued and running work; queued jobs and active Summary/chat jobs can be cancelled, while active native transcription keeps its resource slot until it settles. Duplicate IDs cannot create duplicate requests. Only the latest four completed exchanges enter model context, dropping whole oldest exchanges first when needed. Questions and answers retain canonical Participant labels; display projection uses current names. Ambiguous name mentions are rejected rather than guessed.
- Implemented passage-level FTS5 retrieval is separate from Meeting-list search and scoped to the selected Meeting and current Transcript revision. It returns original fragments: the full Transcript when it fits, otherwise ranked matches with neighbors and late-correction context. One query rewrite can resolve a follow-up. A separate correction-term lookup helps find changes that omit the original topic words. Broad, explicit final/latest/eventual questions and insufficient lookup scan all passages and consolidate original references in bounded requests. Each reduction batch can include the latest two selected original fragments as correction context; context shrinks when needed to fit, without trimming candidate text or deterministically dropping distinct facts. Explicit unassigned or undecided statements support negative answers. Incomplete or cancelled retrieval cannot establish that an answer is absent. No embeddings or vector database are used.
- The shared queue runs one AI job at a time for speech-model preparation, transcription, Summary generation and chat. Detection and Recording remain responsive. Idle speech models are released before Gemma generation; cancellation retains the occupied resource slot until work settles, and shutdown cancels work before closing storage.
- Chat accepts a completed Transcript even when Summary generation failed. Core can also queue chat while only Summary work is pending; its internal authorization to use that Transcript is never renderer-controlled. Recording or pending transcription blocks chat.
- Transcript replacement invalidates its evidence and clears chat. Summary regeneration and Participant renaming preserve chat. Clear chat and Meeting deletion cancel related requests before removing records; revision and request-lifetime checks prevent late output from restoring them. Restart marks unfinished requests interrupted. Deletion blocks new AI requests before audio removal yields, cancels related work and removes all records and retained audio; a failed audio removal restores availability for recovery.

Schema version 3 adds Summary artifacts, derived passages, passage FTS5 and chat requests. The chat table now stores request history, validated answers, evidence references and generation metadata. Passage storage is now wired: existing Transcripts are backfilled on store open and replacements rebuild their derived evidence atomically. No further migration is needed for retrieval. Existing Meetings and Summaries remain readable without regeneration. The UI uses a typed sandboxed preload interface with main-process sender, ID, revision and payload validation. Questions are limited to 2,000 characters in both renderer and main process; generated content is escaped text. Structural and renderer checks do not establish real-Meeting quality or M4 performance.

Markdown exports retain the Summary and Transcript, adding deduplicated supporting timestamps to the TL;DR and each cited point. Current global and per-Meeting Participant names are shown; spoken Transcript words remain unchanged. Legacy Summaries keep their original export format, and invalid or stale evidence is omitted. Export uses timestamps without audio. Chat export and token streaming remain deferred.

The initial model is `gemma3:4b`; at most one alternative, `gemma4:e2b`, is compared on the same examples. Model selection follows measured quality, memory and latency on the target M4 rather than Linux timings. Automated structural validation does not establish factual accuracy; reserved examples need human review. See [the AI brief](./ai-brief.md) for contracts and evaluation gates.

## Out of v1

Live captions, calendar integration, in-person meetings, languages other than English, any cloud processing, cross-Meeting chat, fine-tuning and autonomous actions.

## Verified by the detection prototype

From the `prototype/detection` branch (see `prototypes/detection/FINDINGS.md` there), on macOS 27.0.1:

- Per-process microphone use can be read from CoreAudio process objects (PID, bundle id, input and output running). Reading them needs no permission. A test process produced exactly one start and one stop at a 100 ms poll, with no flapping.
- Browsers and Electron apps run audio in helper processes, so the PID is not the app's. Detection maps the PID to the outermost `.app` in its executable path. This is untested against a real browser call.
- Two-track Recording works: the microphone via AVAudioEngine, system audio via a Core Audio process tap.
- **The two tracks must be aligned on host time, not on the start of the file.** The system track starts about 197 ms before the mic track, repeatably. The engine records each track's first-sample host time and aligns on that before it writes the files: the later track gets leading silence, and both are cut to the same length. `tracks_ready` carries the two start times for reference.
- A Swift helper embeds and runs inside an Electron bundle with identifier `dev.bnd.elegant`.

## Open risks

- Detection maps a process to the `.app` in its executable path and drops processes with none. Safari (WebKit helper services) and FaceTime (system daemons) may fall in that gap. Untested against real calls.
- Which signal each call app produces (Zoom, Teams, a Slack huddle, FaceTime, Meet in a browser), how long the debounce should be, and which things cause false Detections (dictation, Siri, browser voice search). Needs real calls on the machine.
- Whether the embedded helper keeps its microphone and system-audio permissions across relaunch and rebuild. Not yet tested: the bundle's own permission was never exercised, because it could not be launched through LaunchServices and no signing identity exists on the machine. With ad-hoc signing every rebuild probably changes the code requirement and re-prompts, so a stable signing identity is a prerequisite for building the real app.
- Whether the mic and system tracks line up acoustically, not only by host time, and whether that holds on other devices such as Bluetooth headsets.
- Diarization runs on the whole system track as one blend, so two people talking over each other get one label, and a segment that spans a change of speaker is given to one of them. Not yet measured on real Recordings; both models are prepared during setup, with first-use download as a fallback.
- Whether the chosen local model meets long-Meeting factual coverage, attribution and latency gates on the M4. Immutable grouping, whole-claim TL;DR selection and original-sentence selection reduce known failure paths, but initial extraction and semantic judgments remain fallible. Broad scans may abstain or run slowly; real-Meeting review and target-device measurements remain pending. See [`ai-brief.md`](./ai-brief.md) and the versioned evaluation comparisons.
